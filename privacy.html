Privacy Policy

Splitlist · shared to-do lists

Last updated 21 September 2026.

Splitlist is a shared to-do list. Friends join a list, add tasks and check them off. This policy explains exactly what the app stores, why it needs it, who else can see it, how long it is kept and how to get rid of it.

The short version. Splitlist stores your account, your profile, your friendships, your lists and tasks, and the record of who checked what off. It contains no advertising, no analytics and no crash-reporting service, and it does not track you across other apps or websites. It never reads your phone's contacts. You can delete your account from inside the app.

Who is responsible

Splitlist is operated by the independent developer publishing under the name Splitlist, based in Egypt. Splitlist is the party that decides what the app stores and why — in data protection terms, the data controller.

How to contact us: through the public support tracker at github.com/6dzzx4zb7h-afk/splitlist-support/issues. This is the contact route for privacy questions and for the rights described below. Please do not post passwords or other private details there, since the tracker is public.

What Splitlist does not collect

  • No analytics and no crash reporting. The app ships with no such service of any kind.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifier. Nothing is used for targeting and nothing is sold.
  • No tracking across other apps or websites. There is no App Tracking Transparency prompt because there is nothing to ask for.
  • No location, health, financial or browsing data.

Splitlist does not read your contacts

Splitlist never asks for and never accesses your phone's address book. It is not able to: the permission is not requested, and on Android the contacts permissions are explicitly blocked from the app.

Friendships work entirely inside the app. You search for a username that the other person chose and gave you, send a request, and they accept it. Your contacts are never uploaded, scanned or matched against other users.

Photos

If you choose to set a profile picture, the app asks for permission to open your photo library and uploads only the single image you select. It does not browse, scan or upload anything else, and it never uses your camera or microphone.

Uploaded avatars are stored in a publicly readable location. Anyone who has the file's web address can view that image, even without a Splitlist account. Please do not upload a picture you would not be willing to make public. The built-in avatars avoid this entirely.

Who your data is shared with

Splitlist does not sell your data and does not share it with advertisers or data brokers. It is handled by two service providers, who process it only to run the app on our behalf:

Supabase

Supabase provides the database, authentication, file storage and realtime updates. Everything listed in the table above is stored there. The Splitlist database is hosted in Tokyo, Japan (Amazon Web Services region ap-northeast-1). Because the operator is in Egypt and you may be anywhere, your data is transferred internationally to that region and stored there.

See the Supabase privacy policy.

Expo, Apple and Google — only for notifications

If you enable notifications, they are delivered through Expo's push service and from there through Apple's or Google's push network to your device. These providers receive your device's push token and the message.

Push messages deliberately carry only generic text such as "You have a new task assignment" or "A shared task was completed." Task titles, list names and member names are never sent to the push provider, so they do not appear on a lock screen and do not pass through Expo, Apple or Google. The real content stays in the database and is fetched by the app once you open it.

See the Expo privacy policy.

When the law requires it

We may disclose data if legally compelled to, or where it is necessary to protect the rights or safety of users.

What other people can see

  • Your display name, username and avatar are effectively public. Anyone can find you by searching for your username. Treat them as visible to the world.
  • Lists and tasks are visible to current members of that list and to nobody else. This is enforced by database row-level security rules on the server, not merely hidden in the app.
  • Your completion history is visible to members of that list. Showing who did what is the purpose of the product, and it is not something you can hide from other members of a list you have joined.
  • Your email address is not shown to other users.

How long it is kept

Your data is kept for as long as your account exists. There is no automatic expiry, because a to-do list you come back to next year should still be there.

  • Backups: Splitlist runs on a hosting plan that includes no automated database backups. Once data is deleted from the live database it is not retained in a backup copy, and it cannot be restored for you.
  • On your device: the app keeps a copy of recently viewed data on your own phone for up to one day, so it works offline. Signing out deletes that account's cache. A device that is offline cannot learn that your access to a list was revoked until it reconnects.
  • Notification records are tied to the notification and to your device registration, and are removed with your account.

Deleting your account

Open the Settings tab in the app and choose Delete account. This removes your sign-in record, your profile, your friendships, your list memberships, your uploaded avatar file, your notification settings and device tokens, and your personal check history.

Two things are worth stating plainly before you do it:

  • You must first transfer or delete every list you own, including archived ones. The app tells you which lists are blocking the deletion. This exists so that deleting your account cannot destroy a list that other people are still using.
  • Tasks you created inside other people's lists remain, with your authorship anonymised, for the same reason. Removing your checks can reopen tasks that your check had completed.

Deletion is immediate and permanent. As stated above, there is no backup from which it can be undone. If you cannot reach the in-app option, you can request deletion through the support tracker.

Your rights

Different countries grant different rights, and Splitlist has users in many of them. Rather than limit these to one jurisdiction, we extend the following to every user, wherever you live:

  • Access — ask what we hold about you.
  • Correction — your name, username and avatar are editable in the app at any time; ask us about anything else.
  • Deletion — Settings → Delete account, or ask us.
  • Export — ask for a copy of your data in a portable format.
  • Objection and restriction — ask us to stop or limit a particular use.
  • Withdraw consent — turn notifications off at any time in Settings, without losing anything else.
  • Complain — if you are in a country with a data protection authority, you may complain to it. We would rather you told us first.

Depending on where you live these may be legal entitlements — under the EU or UK GDPR, the California CCPA/CPRA, Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law, or a law local to you. Where they are not, we honour them anyway. Make any request through the support tracker.

We do not sell or share personal information as those terms are defined under California law, and we have no financial incentive programme, so there is nothing for you to opt out of.

Why we are allowed to hold it

Where a legal basis is required: we process your account, profile, lists, tasks and completion history because it is necessary to provide the service you asked for, and we process push tokens and notification settings on the basis of your consent, which you give by turning notifications on and withdraw by turning them off.

Children

Splitlist is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from them. If you believe a child under 13 has created an account, tell us through the support tracker and we will delete it.

Security

All traffic between the app and the server is encrypted in transit over HTTPS. Access to lists and tasks is enforced by row-level security rules in the database itself, so a list you are not a member of is not merely hidden from the app — the server refuses to return it. Passwords are handled by Supabase Auth and are never visible to the operator.

No service can promise perfect security, and Splitlist is no exception. Please do not store anything in a task that would be harmful if it were exposed.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and the previous wording remains visible in the public revision history of this page, so you can see exactly what changed and when. Significant changes will also be noted on the support tracker.
